This week's errand

Every video ends with something that isn't about money.

When you live alone, some days the only words you say out loud are to a cashier. So each episode ends with one small, cheap thing that brings you together with someone.

Bake six. Keep three.

Buy the little box of corn muffin mix. Bake the six, keep three, and decide whose door the other three are going to. Bev's go to Gus across the street every Saturday, and if his kitchen light isn't on by eight, she knows to knock.

COST: 67¢ · EPISODE 1

Keep some. Give one away.

Buy yourself one treat that comes in pieces. Keep a few, put the rest in your pocket, and give one to the first person who's kind to you. The young man who lifts the dog food, the woman at the pharmacy who remembers your name.

Meet Bev

From the kitchen table, with a spiral notebook.

Six years ago Bev was still shopping for two and throwing half away. Half a loaf gone green, half a bag of salad gone to soup in the drawer. So she started writing every price down, working out what things cost per use, and learning how to fill a cart for one.

Now she takes that notebook to a different store every week. She'll tell you plainly when the small package is the smarter buy, when your hands deserve the easier one, and why the cheapest thing in her cart is usually the one she'd give up last.

“I'm Bev. Let's get a cart.”

Bev is the host character of Cart for One with Bev, and her stories are written for the show. The prices, products and safety facts she shares are real and checked every month.

Questions

Good to know

Anything else? Ask Bev in the comments on any video.

Are the prices real?

Yes. Every price is checked on the store's own website or app in the week the script is written, and the receipt at the end is the sum of those prices. They vary by store and change over time, so check yours.

Does Bev give health or money advice?

No. Bev talks about grocery prices, storing food and staying safe at home. For salt, sugar, medicines or a health condition, she'll always tell you to ask your doctor or pharmacist. There's no advice on benefits, insurance or investing.

Where do the safety tips come from?

Food-safety, fall and fire tips are checked against official sources such as the USDA, the CDC and the U.S. Fire Administration, and the sources are listed with every video.

How often are new videos?

Every week. Each one walks a store or a kitchen topic, with 10 to 15 buys, 3 to 5 put-backs and a receipt at the end.

What's in the ebook that isn't in the videos?

The ebook pulls the method together in one place: how to read a shelf tag, portion and freeze, the fridge and freezer charts, a two-week supper plan, a power-outage checklist, a daily check-in plan, 20 recipes for one and 8 printable pages. It teaches the method, not this week's prices.
